# OTCS-0015 — The bright line: standing to object is never a proposal topic

**Class:** `constitutional` · **Phase:** DRAFT · **Clock start:** 2026-09-20
**Clock:** 45–90 days (`GOVERNANCE.md` §3, changing the rules themselves) — **earliest ratification: clock start + 45 days**

## The sentence

Added to `CHARTER.md` §5, after the canonical voting principle:

> **A proposal whose effect is to deny any class of participants the ability to object is refused at SEED.** The process may decide anything else. It may not be used to decide who may use it.

## Why it needs to exist before it is needed

`CHARTER.md` §5 says every participant has immediate voice. `GOVERNANCE.md` §2 says anyone may open a SEED. `VOTING.md` §5 says arriving early earns provenance, not sovereignty. Nothing says that a proposal to remove a class of participants' voice is out of order, and a registry that intends to hold laws and policies will be asked to hold exactly that proposal. Equal time in a deliberation presumes the voice counts; a process that can vote a voice out of existence has no floor. Saying so in one sentence now is cheaper than litigating it once.

## What it does not do

It does not protect any position, any project, or any claim from objection. It protects the ability to object. Standing to *vote* is still earned by trail (`VOTING.md` §2–3); standing to *speak* is what this sentence puts beyond the process's reach.

## Alternatives considered

- **Bundle with OTCS-0013.** Rejected: a constitution's one bright line should not be hostage to a schema debate about objection fields.
- **`NON-GOALS.md` as a twenty-first entry.** Rejected: that document says what OTCS is not; this says what its process may not decide.
- **`BOUNDARY-STATEMENTS.md`.** Rejected: that page is builder-facing and about where a system stops.

## On decision

If ratified: the sentence enters `CHARTER.md` §5 and `GOVERNANCE.md` §2's SEED row cites it. If rejected: the absence is recorded in `CHARTER.md` §12, *How this could fail*, as a known failure mode.
