## Why it belongs in this deliberation

This proposal (OTCS-0004) exists because a registry entry fell by default
into a licence broader than the registry needs. The exhibit shows the
mature form of that default, operating today in a respected registry:

- **The public lane is non-commercial for everyone; the operators hold the
  only commercial key.** The NC licence creates the scarcity; the grant lets
  exactly two named parties sell the exception. Any fork inherits NC, so no
  competing commercial use can ever exist — enclosure achieved through
  licensing alone.
- **`transferable`** — an acquirer of the operating companies inherits the
  grant to every contribution ever made.
- **Retroactive** — the grant covers contributions submitted before the
  current terms were written.
- **Moral rights waived** — contributors agree not to object to modification
  or use on authorship grounds.

Run those defaults over *this* registry's cargo — owners describing their own
work, sometimes unpublished architectures — and every entry becomes an asset
the operator can sell, forever, and hand to an acquirer. That is the precise
shape a prospective registrant named as their condition for not
participating, and the shape this proposal inverts: the entry is licensed by
its author, `all-rights-reserved` is a first-class choice, and registration
grants only an enumerated operating minimum — store, validate, publish
verbatim, mirror, archive, derive registry views — with no sublicensing,
nothing transferable, and no commercial grant.

## Fairness, stated plainly

Nothing here criticises the catalog. Its terms are disclosed, its cargo is
low-stakes (third parties curating public facts about vendors' products, not
owners filing their own work), and dual licensing is a legitimate,
long-standing operating model. The exhibit's value is exactly that it is
**reasonable for their cargo and disqualifying for ours** — which is the
clearest available demonstration that entry licensing is a design decision a
registry must make deliberately, not inherit from convention.
